# Nondegenerate symmetric bilinear underdetermination

Let V = Q^2 with coordinate lines C+ = span(e1) and C- = span(e2). Consider

P0 = [[1, 0], [0, 1]],
P2 = [[1, 2], [2, 1]].

Both forms are symmetric. Their determinants are 1 and -3, so both are nondegenerate over Q. Their restrictions to C+ and C- are each the one-dimensional form [1]. Their mixed terms differ: P0(e1,e2)=0 while P2(e1,e2)=2.

Therefore, even inside the class of nondegenerate symmetric bilinear forms on Q^2, the two diagonal chart restrictions do not determine the mixed transfer.

This theorem does not prove that either form is an admissible Orthad pairing. It proves only the dependency statement used here: diagonal restrictions alone are insufficient.
